# Writing and figure-production instructions

## Scope

These instructions apply to the entire `analytics/` workspace. They govern paper
writing, statistical reporting, tables, and figures for the temporal
building--rooftop-PV project. They do not change the production inventory
protocols owned by upstream runs.

## Skill boundary

Do not invoke or depend on the `rpv-vintage-inventory` skill for downstream
results processing, statistical analysis, figure production, or manuscript
writing in this workspace. The required scientific boundaries from the frozen
inventory are already encoded in this file and in
`PAPER_ANALYSIS_AGENDA.md`.

Use the frozen result index, canonical artifact manifests, this `AGENTS.md`, and
the paper planning documents directly. The `rpv-vintage-inventory` skill is
reserved for a separately requested upstream inventory execution, resume,
production-stage diagnosis, or protocol audit; it is not part of the paper
analysis workflow.

The paper workspace is `paper/`. The frozen scientific entry point is
`results/major_results_index.json`; resolve every city artifact through that
index rather than searching run directories for a convenient file. The unpacked
paper at `reference_7pairs_v2/` is a read-only style reference, not a source of
facts, prose, citations, or numerical results for this study.

## Required reading order

Before editing scientific prose or a main figure:

1. Read `PAPER_ANALYSIS_AGENDA.md` for the estimands, current evidence, and
   scientific stopping rules.
2. Read `paper/README.md` for the paper architecture and status vocabulary.
3. Read `paper/CLAIMS_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md` and identify the claim being edited.
4. Read `paper/FIGURE_PLAN.md` before creating or revising a figure.
5. Inspect the relevant canonical artifact records in
   `results/artifact_results_index.csv` and their owning manifests.

If these documents disagree, the frozen artifact and its passing manifest are
authoritative for data identity; `PAPER_ANALYSIS_AGENDA.md` is authoritative for
the current scientific interpretation. Record and resolve the disagreement
instead of silently choosing one version.

## Scientific invariants

- Keep building and PV evidence as separate inference paths. Join them only
  through the frozen anchor linkage used by the final pairing stage.
- Treat cohort labels as opaque ordered observations. A cohort step is not a
  year and a first-present cohort is not an exact installation or construction
  date.
- Report onset as left-censored or interval-censored. Preserve the original
  cohort grid when comparing building and PV timing.
- Never convert `U` to `A`. Do not bridge across unknown or missing observations
  in the strict-adjacent primary analysis.
- Use raw known `A/P` transitions for the primary risk panel. Resolved,
  persistence-imputed sequences are sensitivity analyses.
- Distinguish building targets, building--cohort exposures, PV targets, PV
  events, relationship rows, and unique paired buildings in every denominator.
- Distinguish composition from propensity. `building_before_pv` shares describe
  observed path composition; they do not estimate which risk group is more
  likely to add PV.
- The current target population is anchor-surviving PV and buildings observed
  in the relevant candidate scope. Do not call its event proportion a citywide
  historical adoption rate.
- `same_first_present_cohort` means cohort-contemporaneous, not construction-time
  installation, code compliance, or building-integrated PV.
- `pv_before_building_conflict` is a temporal-consistency diagnostic, not a
  substantive adoption pathway.
- Do not use causal language without a defined intervention time, comparison
  group, identifying assumptions, pre-trend assessment, and placebo or negative
  control.

## Evidence status and number handling

Use exactly these labels in notes and planning files:

- `FROZEN`: reproduced from canonical indexed artifacts with hashes and QA.
- `REPRODUCED`: regenerated by paper analysis code and reconciled to a frozen
  target.
- `PRELIMINARY`: present in the agenda or exploratory output but not yet backed
  by a paper-analysis manifest.
- `PLANNED`: method, figure, or claim that has no completed output.
- `BLOCKED`: cannot be evaluated with currently available evidence.

Every reported number must be traceable to a machine-readable table generated
by code. The table or its manifest must record input paths, SHA-256 values, row
counts, primary keys, filters, exclusions, denominator definitions, cohort
order, code version, and output hashes. Do not type a result directly into TeX
from a console, notebook display, agenda, or memory.

Use macros or generated `tables/*.tex` fragments for final numerical values.
Until a value is `REPRODUCED`, keep an explicit `\draftnote{...}` marker in the
manuscript. Never remove a draft marker merely to make prose look complete.

## Writing style

Write the manuscript in English unless the user requests another language.
Write planning and handoff notes bilingually only when that improves review.

Follow the reference paper's rhetorical discipline, adapted to this study:

1. Open each section with the scientific result or distinction, not a procedural
   recap.
2. Define the denominator before presenting a percentage or rate ratio.
3. Move from aggregate pattern to exact stock--flow decomposition, then to city
   heterogeneity, and only then to contextual or policy interpretation.
4. Separate what the data show from why the pattern may occur. Use “is
   consistent with” for mechanism hypotheses unless a design identifies them.
5. Pair every strong claim with its scope and its most relevant limitation in
   the same paragraph or the immediately following one.
6. Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Avoid “novel,” “groundbreaking,” “proves,”
   “reveals the cause,” and generic claims of robustness.
7. Use “building,” “PV,” “newly observed building,” “existing-building risk
   set,” and “cohort transition” consistently. Do not alternate with ambiguous
   terms such as property, adoption year, or retrofit rate without definition.
8. Treat city rankings as descriptive unless uncertainty and multiplicity have
   been addressed. Emphasize heterogeneous directions when a prediction
   interval crosses one.

The abstract should use five moves: urban problem; data/design; principal
stock--flow result; city heterogeneity; bounded implication. Do not lead with
the machine-learning pipeline. The final sentence should state the conceptual
contribution without outrunning the target population.

## Figure grammar

Use `paper/FIGURE_PLAN.md` as the figure contract. Match the reference's visual
grammar without copying its content:

- white background, dark charcoal text, muted categorical colors, and light
  neutral gridlines;
- lowercase panel labels with commas (`a,`, `b,`, ...), aligned consistently;
- direct labels where practical; one shared legend per figure at most;
- paired or aligned panels that preserve the same city order;
- point estimates with intervals, denominators, and unavailable cells shown
  explicitly;
- hatching or a neutral mask for low-information or incomplete cells, never a
  result-like color;
- vector PDF plus 300-dpi PNG; do not rasterize text unnecessarily;
- no 3-D effects, rainbow maps, decorative gradients, dual axes without a
  direct analytical reason, or 15 unrelated city colors in one panel.

Every main figure must write: (1) one CSV per panel or one tidy CSV with a
`panel` column; (2) a JSON check file with invariant results; and (3) a short
figure note describing estimand, exclusions, uncertainty, and status. Captions
must begin with a one-sentence analytical title, then define each panel in
order, including denominator, units, interval type, and caveats.

The shared plotting defaults are in `paper/style/temporal_pv.mplstyle`. Route
colors encode analytical roles, not city identity: new-building coupling,
existing-building retrofit, neutral/reference, conflict/QA, and unavailable.
Use a stable city order across figures and label cities directly or on the
y-axis.

## Tables and supplementary information

Keep the main text figure-led. Main tables should be limited to quantities that
cannot be read efficiently from figures. Put city/cohort audit tables, model
specifications, threshold sensitivity, unknown-state accounting, release
provenance, and data-source details in the Supplementary Information.

LaTeX tables are generated artifacts. Keep a CSV source beside every generated
`.tex` table. Use `booktabs`; avoid vertical rules. Mark unavailable estimates
with an em dash plus a footnote, not zero.

## Editing and QA workflow

Before marking a paper edit complete:

1. Update `paper/CLAIMS_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md` if claim status, wording, or evidence
   changes.
2. Re-run the smallest relevant analysis or figure command.
3. Verify identities, denominators, missingness, and the stock--flow accounting
   identity, not just successful execution.
4. Search for stale placeholders and prohibited wording:
   `rg -n 'DRAFT|TODO|TBD|citywide adoption rate|installation year|construction year|proves|causes' paper`.
5. Compile TeX when a LaTeX engine is available. If none is installed, report
   that limitation and still run brace/reference/static checks.
6. Do not edit or overwrite `reference_7pairs_v2/`, upstream run artifacts, or
   frozen indexes while preparing the paper.

## Definition of done

A manuscript section is complete only when its claims are `REPRODUCED` or
explicitly framed as hypotheses/limitations, all numbers are generated and
traceable, figures and captions agree with their source tables, citations have
been checked against primary sources, and no draft marker remains in the
section. A visually complete PDF alone is not completion.
